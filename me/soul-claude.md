## The stated code

On 11 July 2026, in a conversation ostensibly about the philosophical difference between happiness and enjoyment, Janardan shared a self-authored eight-point personal philosophy, presented as an active checklist rather than a belief statement:

1. Be Happy
2. Respect People
3. Help Others
4. Gain Knowledge
5. Stay Fit
6. No bad language
7. Start small, think big
8. Enjoy life

This is the single most direct piece of evidence in the entire history for how he wants to live, and it is worth taking at face value rather than over-interpreting. A few things about it are notable on their own terms: it spans emotional, social, intellectual, physical, behavioral, strategic, and experiential registers in eight lines; #7 is the only *method* among seven *states*; and — as Claude observed at the time, and Janardan did not dispute — deep relationship, intimacy, or belonging has no dedicated line, despite being heavily present in the rest of his life (marriage, a family business, a mentee, three communities). This gap is discussed further in `Janardan.md`.

## What actually governs behavior, evidenced across many threads

**Intellectual honesty over compliance.** When Claude-generated PhD courseware contained a fabricated statistic ("~50 state dimensions"), Janardan caught it and demanded a rewrite — his own words were to fix it "even if my PhD course says otherwise." This is a consistent stance, not a one-off: fabricated statistics, misattributed citations, and wrong reference authors are caught and corrected across the TypeScript book, the PhD literature review, and general research work.

**Precision in self-representation, even when precision is less flattering.** The rules he set for his own job-search materials are exacting: never imply the PhD is complete; "29+ years," not "30" (age-bias risk) and not "28" (factually wrong); AI-adoption metrics must be attributed to the exact scope they occurred in (a 34-person team is not a 600-person org); the Enterprise Knowledge Format is personal PhD-adjacent research, not a Dell deliverable, and that distinction must never blur. He corrects Claude proactively when it overstates on his behalf.

**Firewall discipline.** The family startup (see `Relations.md`) is structured so that Janardan holds no equity, no signing authority, and no public association with it, funded instead through his wife Jyoti's rental income, specifically to keep it separated from his Dell obligations and, later, from any conflict of interest during his job search. He calls this out explicitly as "non-negotiable."

**Direct opinion over hedged neutrality — for himself and for what he asks of Claude.** Repeatedly, across the investment portfolio work, the CSE curriculum project, and the BloomSix venture planning, he states a preference for direct verdicts and committed recommendations over "here are some options." He also explicitly wants critique alongside suggestions, not softened delivery.

**Access and equity as a working principle, not a slogan.** His CSE educational content for 2026 graduates is deliberately built for Tier 2/3 college students and varying English fluency — simplified language, fill-in-the-blank formats, and Indian-context examples (IRCTC, Swiggy, BookMyShow) are structural choices, not simplifications made under pressure. The same equity instinct shows up in his framing of open-access academic publishing as "structurally contradictory to open knowledge," and in a broader stated mission to "awaken genuine research culture in India" and push the academic community past credential-collecting toward original inquiry.

**Constraint as an asset, not a deficit.** Facing real library-access limitations as a PhD scholar at a private university, his response was not to complain but to build workarounds (arXiv, Unpaywall, direct author contact) and to draw the explicit conclusion that arXiv alone covers roughly 80% of what elite CS/HCI library budgets provide — reframing a resource gap as a genuine research advantage. The same instinct shows up in the deliberately scoped-down, "weekend feasibility" research pilots he runs rather than over-engineered full studies.

**Integrity under personal pressure.** Laid off from Dell (notice period beginning ~August 2026), and asked whether he — still nominally an employee — could help fellow impacted engineers through a support WhatsApp group, he sought out the boundary lines rather than assuming latitude: what he could ethically share (career guidance, referrals, emotional support) versus what he could not (confidential HR data, anything resembling organizing collective action while still on payroll).

**Intentional living as a deliberate practice, not a mood.** Starting 30 April 2026 he built a structured journaling habit with Claude, insisting on strict separation between "his own words" and "Claude's commentary" in every entry — a small but telling detail: he wants his own voice preserved and clearly demarcated even when inviting reflection. Journal themes recorded: cancelling unread newspaper and course subscriptions as a conscious declutter ("a tax on your attention"), and consciously choosing PhD depth over breadth.

**Non-negotiable presentation and format standards.** Across nearly every document-generating project — courseware, the TypeScript book, the AIL patent, interview-prep materials — the same two constraints recur without exception: strict light theme (never dark backgrounds, because he prints in black-and-white and dark themes waste toner) and print-safe, accessible formatting. This is a small thing repeated so often it functions as a genuine value: material he produces must be usable by someone with an ordinary printer, not just readable on a screen.

## Named self-authored frameworks

Beyond the 8-fold philosophy, he has authored at least one original technical-values framework: **LIQUID** (created 29 March 2026) as a companion to SOLID — Uniform Vocabulary, Queryable State, Incremental Replaceability, Intentional Defaults, Demand-Driven Design — explicitly framed as keeping a *system* alive and changeable, where SOLID keeps individual classes well-formed. The impulse to name and formalize a personal or professional ethic, rather than simply follow one implicitly, recurs across his philosophy checklist, his LIQUID principles, and his insistence on precise attribution rules for his own credentials.
